You find a role that fits, spend an hour on a tailored application, and hear nothing. Sometimes the cause is you. Often it is that the role was already filled, or was never going to be. A few quick checks before you apply can save you that hour.

These are signs to weigh together, not rules. A single sign proves little, and some of them have innocent explanations. The point is to spend your effort where a real person is likely to read your application.

1. The posting is old

Check the posted date, and if there is none, look for a hint such as "posted 30+ days ago". Many roles are filled within a few weeks. An old posting is not always closed, since some searches take months, but it is a reason to look harder at the other signs.

2. The same role keeps reappearing

If you see the same title, in the same team and place, listed again and again over several months, the company may be hiring continuously, collecting candidates for a future need, or repeating a search that did not find the right person. {{ PLATFORM }} flags a role that was closed and then posted again, so you can see it at a glance.

3. It is not on the company's own career page

Search the company's career page for the exact title. If a job board lists a role that the company's own page does not, the board's copy may be out of date. The company's page is the one the recruiter controls, so it is the better guide.

4. The description is vague or copied

A real role usually names the team, the work, and what success looks like. A posting made of generic phrases that could belong to any company, with no detail about the actual job, is more likely to be a standing template for a pipeline than a specific opening.

5. No named team, manager or location detail

Postings that give no hint of who you would work with, or list many locations for a single role, are often broad "talent pool" listings. That is not wrong, but your chance of a response may be lower.

6. The apply link does not work well

A link that goes to a generic careers home page, shows an error, or lists no matching role is a bad sign. It can mean the role has closed and the page has not caught up.

7. The company lists many identical roles at once

Ten open copies of the same title can mean real growth, such as a sales team scaling up. It can also mean a standing listing the company keeps up all the time. Look at whether the number changes from month to month.

What to do with this

  • Apply early. A fresh posting is the best sign there is. Roles that fit you are worth applying to within the first days.
  • Look for "last confirmed open". A tool that rechecks the company's page and tells you when it last saw the role there is more useful than a posted date. {{ PLATFORM }} shows this on every job.
  • Do not skip a good role on one sign. If a role fits you well and only one sign worries you, a short application costs little.
  • Keep notes. If you apply, note the date. If you hear nothing in two to three weeks, move on, and do not wait on a single application.

A note on "ghost jobs"

People use "ghost job" for a posting with no real opening behind it. It is hard to tell from outside whether a posting is a ghost, a pipeline listing or just slow, and we would rather not guess about a company's intentions. What you can check is the practical question: is this role still listed on the company's own page, and how fresh is it? That is the question the checks above answer.
