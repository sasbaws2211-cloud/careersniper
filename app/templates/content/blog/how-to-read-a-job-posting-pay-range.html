More job postings now state what the role pays, because some places require it and many employers have chosen to. A range such as $90,000 to $120,000 is useful, but only if you know what it stands for.

What a posted range usually is

A pay range is normally the band the employer has set for the role. It is not a promise, and it is not a guess at what the market pays. It is what the company is prepared to offer for this job at this level. Rules about publishing ranges differ from place to place, so check what applies where you live, but the meaning of the number is the same wherever it appears.

Why ranges can be wide

  • One role, several levels. A band may cover people at different experience levels. The bottom is for someone who just meets the requirements, the top for someone well beyond them.
  • Different locations. Some employers pay by region, so one posting can show the range for the highest-cost location.
  • Room for negotiation. A wide range gives the company space to adjust to the candidate.

Where you are likely to land

There is no formula, but a few rules of thumb are reasonable. If you meet the requirements and little more, expect the lower half. If you have clear experience beyond the posting, and you can show it, aim for the middle or higher. The very top is usually kept for rare experience or a strong competing offer. If a range is unusually wide, ask the recruiter early which part of it applies to the level they are hiring for.

Check what the number includes

  • Base only, or total pay? Many ranges show base salary only. Bonus, commission and equity are often separate and can be a large part of the package.
  • The period. Check whether the figure is per hour, per month or per year, and the currency.
  • Full-time or part-time. Part-time or contract roles may state a rate that is not comparable with a full-time salary.

Use the range, and more than one range

A single posting tells you what one company offers. To see what a role pays across employers, look at several postings for the same title. {{ PLATFORM }} builds salary pages from the ranges employers have published on their own career pages, and shows the typical band (the middle half of the ranges) for each role, in US dollars a year. Treat these as a guide to what employers are advertising, not a promise of what you will be paid.

Using the range when you negotiate

  1. Know the band before the call. If the posting shows a range, you do not need to guess.
  2. Anchor in the range, not below it. If you are asked what you expect, give a figure inside the band that you can justify, and say what it is based on.
  3. Talk about the whole package. If base pay is fixed, ask about bonus, equity, leave, remote work or a review after six months.
  4. Ask what level the role is hired at. The same title can sit at different levels and bands.

When there is no range

Not every posting states pay. {{ PLATFORM }} shows "No pay listed" in that case, and you can filter for jobs that do list it. If a role you like has no range, research similar roles, and ask for the range at the start of the process so that you do not discover a gap after several interviews.
