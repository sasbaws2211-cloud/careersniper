When you click "Careers" on a company's website, you often land on a page that looks like the company's own but is run by a hiring system. Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby are three of the best known. Knowing how they work helps you find roles that other sites miss.

What an applicant tracking system does

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is software that a company uses to manage hiring: it publishes job postings, collects applications, and lets recruiters and hiring managers review candidates and schedule interviews. Many of these systems also give each company a public job board, a web page listing every open role with an apply button.

That public board is the page you see when you apply. It is also where the posting starts: the company writes the role in its ATS, and the ATS publishes it to the company's board, and, if the company chooses, sends it on to job boards.

Why a role can be on a company board and not on LinkedIn

Sending a role to other job boards is a separate choice, and some boards charge employers to post or to promote a role. A company may send some roles to some boards, or none at all. Even when a role is sent on, the copy can appear later than the original, or stay up after the original has closed. So the company's own board is usually the most accurate view.

How to tell which system a company uses

  • Click Apply on any role and look at the address in the browser bar. The system's name is usually in it.
  • Look at the page footer. Many boards say "Powered by Greenhouse" or similar.
  • Search for the company name together with a pattern from the table, for example "acme" site:jobs.lever.co.

What is different between them

For a job seeker the boards look much alike: a list of roles, filters for team and location, and an apply form. The differences are behind the scenes: how the company handles interview scheduling, scorecards and reporting. What matters to you is practical: some boards show pay ranges, some show when a role was posted, and some do neither. When a board shows a posted date, use it.

Searching many boards at once

A single board is easy to read, but you rarely care about one company.
