How this works

We read the job description and pick out its most important words and phrases: skills, tools and the terms it repeats, giving extra weight to anything under a “Requirements” heading. Then we check which of them appear in your résumé, treating different forms of a word as the same (“managed” and “managing”) and common short forms alike (“k8s” and “Kubernetes”).

The check is plain text matching, so it is free, instant and gives the same answer every time. Treat it as a prompt to look again, not as a verdict. Never add a skill you do not have just to raise the score.

Questions

Do you keep my résumé or the job description?

No. They are used to work out the answer and are not saved{% if ai_on %}. If you ask for AI advice, the two texts are sent to an AI service to write it, so leave out anything you would not want shared{% endif %}.

What is a good score?

Above about 70% means most of the job’s wording is already in your résumé. A lower score is common when a job description includes a lot of general wording. What matters more is whether the important, specific skills are covered.

Does a high score guarantee an interview?

No. Employers use many different systems and people decide in many ways. This tells you about wording only.
